# Standpoint Labs Homepage Narrative (Hybrid A+B)

**Created:** 20260612
**Status:** Draft for reaction, not implementation
**Direction:** Lead with mission-led teams (A), dedicated section for sovereign agent stacks (B), communities folded in as use case (C)

---

## The Spine (one sentence everything hangs from)

Small groups where relationships are load-bearing, human or agent, need infrastructure that models what connections mean.

---

## Section 1: Hero

Three options, strongest first in my view:

**Option 1**
> # Your Team Runs on Relationships. Your Tools Can't See Them.
> Subhead: Standpoint maps what every connection means, to each person, from each side. Built for mission-led teams of 5 to 50, and the agents working alongside them.

**Option 2**
> # AI Agents Are Everywhere. Meaning Isn't.
> Subhead: Agents have your data. They don't know what it means to the people involved. We build the relational layer that closes that gap.
> (Keeps continuity with the current headline rhythm. More B-weighted.)

**Option 3**
> # Every Team Has a Map No One Can See.
> Subhead: Who holds what. Who depends on whom. What breaks when something changes. Standpoint makes the invisible map explicit, and keeps it on infrastructure you control.

Descriptor line (replaces "Coordination infrastructure for perspective-aware AI"):
> Relational infrastructure for sovereign teams.

---

## Section 2: The Problem, Rewritten at 5-50 Scale

The Sarah/VP scenario is enterprise furniture. Same cascade structure, new scale:

> ## One Resignation. A 14-Person Company. Five Quiet Cascades.
>
> Jordan gives notice. Fully understandable, well handled, two weeks.
>
> But in a small company, everyone is load-bearing:
>
> - The **Meridian account** trusts Jordan, not the company
> - **Onboarding** lives in Jordan's head, not the wiki
> - **Sam's growth** ran through Jordan's mentorship
> - The **vendor relationship** that keeps margins workable? Jordan's
> - The **Q3 launch** assumed Jordan's sign-off speed
>
> Month 2: Meridian goes quiet. Month 4: Sam is interviewing. Month 5: the launch slips.
>
> At enterprise scale there's redundancy. At your scale, there isn't.
>
> **This is the perspective problem. We solve it.**

Why this works: enterprise buyers read the original scenario as a process failure. Small-team leaders read this one as a memory of something that actually happened to them.

---

## Section 3: The Sovereign Agent Section (the B page-within-a-page)

Replaces "The Agent Babysitter Problem." Speaks directly to the self-hosted harness wave without naming-and-depending on any single project:

> ## You Made Your Agents Sovereign. Now Make Them Coordinate.
>
> The self-hosted agent wave is real. Agents on your own server, your own models, your own data. No cloud lock-in. That part is solved.
>
> What isn't solved: your agents are islands. Each one acts without understanding what its actions mean to the others, or to the people they serve. They have context. They don't have meaning.
>
> Sovereignty without coordination is just lonelier chaos.
>
> **We're building the relational layer for sovereign agent stacks.**

Mesh hint (soft, no commitment):

> Core gives agents perspective today. The layer that lets whole agent stacks share context across boundaries, without sharing everything, is taking shape in the open. [Follow the build](#follow).
