**Created:** 20260612
**Status:** Draft for reaction, not implementation
**Direction:** Infrastructure positioning. Core + Mesh as the relational layer; partners build applications; sovereignty as trust guarantee, not ideology.

## The Spine

Anyone trying to serve people well, whether an app, a leader, a coach, or an agent, needs to understand what things mean from each side. Standpoint builds that layer.

## Section 2: Why "Standpoint"

Short section, almost a manifesto beat. The company name is the plain-English version of the concept:

> Every relationship looks different depending on where you stand.
>
> A promotion means opportunity to one person, loss to another, risk to a customer who trusted the old structure. The same connection never means the same thing twice.
>
> Most systems hold one view and call it truth. We map the standpoints.

This carries what "multi-perspective relational awareness" means without the jargon.

---

## Section 3: We Build the Layer, Not the Apps

The stack, drawn plainly:

> **Applications** (built by partners): community intelligence, coaching and growth tools, relationship repair, decision and stakeholder mapping, agent experiences
>
> **Relational infrastructure** (Standpoint Labs): Core maps what connections mean, to whom, and why. Mesh will let systems act on those standpoints together.
>
> **Sovereign hardware** (in partnership with Oznog): infrastructure you control, or a steward runs for you
>
> We don't build the applications. We build the layer they need and can't easily build themselves.

Open intent statement lives here:
> We're building in the open, with the intent that this layer belongs to the people who depend on it, not to a silo.

(Statement of direction, deliberately not a license commitment yet.)

---

## Section 4: What Gets Built on Core (three vignettes)

> **A members club that actually connects people.** An app surfaces the right introduction at the right moment, because the layer beneath it knows what each member seeks, offers, and protects. Genuine opportunity instead of networking noise.
>
> **A decision that doesn't cascade blind.** In a 14-person company, everyone is load-bearing. A planning tool shows what one resignation means to each client, teammate, and project before the dominoes fall, not after.
>
> **Agents that stay in alignment.** An agent acting for you should understand what its actions mean to the people involved. Prompts and rules are fragile. Understanding holds.

Each vignette ends implied, not stated: "this is an application; Core is what makes it possible."

## Section 5: The First Deployment Is Me

The dogfooding and trust section, replacing generic "About" credibility-only framing:

> I'm building the first complete deployment around myself. Years of writing, full social media archives, thousands of hours of AI collaboration transcripts, all of it mapped: gifts, weaknesses, desires, vulnerabilities, projects, vision. So the people and digital intelligences working with me can align with who I actually am, beyond the fragile rules I'd otherwise have to write.
>
> A map like that is the most valuable dataset that exists about a person. It is also the most dangerous. The same understanding that helps someone serve you could be used against you.
>
> That's why this data lives on hardware I control, not in someone else's cloud. Sovereignty isn't ideology here. It's the only responsible way to hold this kind of map.

Founder bio (G-Log/OTM, MavenWire) follows in compressed form. Oznog partnership paragraph stays.

## Section 6: The Sovereign Agent Wave (B thread, updated)

> The self-hosted agent wave is real. Agents on your own server, your own models, your own data. That part is solved.
>
> What isn't solved: agents are islands. They have your data without your meaning. Each one acts without understanding what its actions imply for the others, or for the people they serve.
>
> Core gives agents perspective today. The layer that lets whole agent stacks share context across boundaries, without sharing everything, is taking shape in the open. Follow the build.

Mesh stays future-tense, hinted not committed.

## Section 7: Who This Is For (re-tiered)

> **Builders and product teams.** You're building community intelligence, coaching tools, decision software, or agent experiences. Build on a relational layer you don't have to invent.
>
> **Mission-led organizations and communities.** 5 to 50 people where every relationship is load-bearing. Become a design partner or a stewarded deployment, and shape what this becomes.
>
> **Sovereign agent builders.** You run your own stack. Give it the layer it's missing.

Two-track ask, stated plainly:
> Build on this, or deploy this with us. Two more design partners. That's the current ask.
